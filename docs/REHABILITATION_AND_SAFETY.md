# Rehabilitation, Return to Sport, and Safety

## Intended role

TwinStride AI is a decision-support tool that can organise evidence, track change, and support professional discussion. It does not independently diagnose injuries, prescribe unsupervised rehabilitation, or clear an athlete for competition.

## Configurable workflow

A clinician may configure stages appropriate to the athlete, injury, and sport. A generic sprinting workflow might include:

1. Movement reintroduction
2. Controlled running
3. Acceleration and submaximal sprinting
4. High-speed sprint exposure
5. Return to training and competition

These are workflow labels, not a universal clinical protocol. Timing, criteria, and progression must be injury-specific and set by qualified professionals.

## Evidence that may be considered

- Symptoms and response during and after loading
- Clinician-assessed strength and range of motion
- Relevant functional test results
- Running speed, repetitions, distance, rest, and workload tolerance
- Movement trends under comparable capture conditions
- Athlete confidence and psychological readiness
- Sport-specific demands and multidisciplinary input

A failed or missing criterion should trigger review or a hold according to the clinician's protocol, not an automatic diagnosis or an instruction to train through symptoms.

## Human decision controls

- Clinical criteria are versioned and attributed to an authorised clinician.
- AI suggestions are not clearance decisions.
- Return-to-sport clearance must be explicitly recorded by an authorised professional.
- Preserve the evidence and rationale available at the time of each decision.
- Allow clinicians to override suggestions and record their rationale.
- Never silently rewrite historical decisions after a model update.

## Privacy and governance

Athlete videos and injury records are sensitive. Implement explicit consent, role-based access, encryption, audit logs, retention and deletion policies, and secure separation of research data. Review compliance with South Africa's POPIA and other applicable jurisdictions before deployment.

## Claims policy

Do not market the MVP as predicting or preventing injuries without appropriate validation for the specific claim. Movement deviations are signals to investigate, not proof an injury will occur. Avoid a single universal injury-risk score in the initial release.
